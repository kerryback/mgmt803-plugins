# Plan format

The plan is a proposal. Say at the top that **nothing has been written to Trello or the calendar yet**.

## 1. How the dates were built

- The anchors the user confirmed (term windows, session days).
- Anything the syllabi don't give. If a syllabus has no usable calendar dates at all, say so directly.
- **Working assumptions**, one per line, each with the dates it produces. Add: "Change any of these and everything downstream recalculates."
- The lead-time policy applied, and where a short window forced a smaller buffer.

## 2. Confirmations needed before anything is created

A numbered table with these columns: #, What I need, Why it matters. **Order it by how much downstream damage a wrong answer would cause.** A session weekday that every date depends on goes first, and a missing survey link goes last.

## 3. One section per course

Start with a heading line: the course, the Trello list name, "N cards to create, M on hold", and the date range.

Then the card table:

| Proposed card | Type | Official deadline | Proposed target | Notes / flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Type** is a short label, such as Reading, Prep, Async prep, Assignment, Graded (individual or group), Discussion, Exam or Materials.
- **Official deadline** gives the syllabus wording, then the derived date in parentheses, or "NO DATE GIVEN".
- **Notes / flags** gives the deliverable details, "FLAG:" for anything uncertain, "My addition" for implied cards, and "HOLD — do not create without your approval" for undated graded items.

End each course with a one-to-three-sentence **structural note** covering the grade weighting, the rhythm of the course, and where the real risk sits.

## 4. Workload collisions

Name the crunch weeks across all courses and list what lands in each. Say which confirmation, if any, would ease them.

## 5. Deliberately not carded

List each thing left off the board, and say where it went instead (the calendar, a card description or a checklist) or why it needs nothing.

## 6. Draft caveat

Include this only if any syllabus is a draft or looks outdated. Say what looks stable (weights, deliverables, page counts, the weekly rhythm) and what doesn't (specific dates). Recommend creating the cards against the derived dates now and re-checking them against the final syllabus in the first week of term.
